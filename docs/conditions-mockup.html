Alpha-gal syndrome

Alpha-gal syndrome is an allergy to a sugar called galactose-α-1,3-galactose — found in most mammals, but not in people. In the US it usually begins after a bite from a lone star tick. Your immune system starts treating that sugar as a threat, so beef, pork, lamb and venison can set off a reaction. The timing is what throws people: symptoms often arrive two to six hours after eating, so the meal rarely gets blamed.

Your signals
Alpha-gal IgE 4.2 kU/L Total IgE 88 kU/L Tryptase 4.1 µg/L

From your bloodwork uploaded Jun 14.

This one belongs with an allergist
Alpha-gal reactions can be severe, and diagnosis needs a blood test read against your history. Everything below supports that care — it doesn't stand in for it.
The protocol
1
Confirm it with an allergist — talk to your doctor first
Alpha-gal is diagnosed with a specific IgE blood test read alongside what actually happened to you. This is a diagnosis to confirm, not one to settle on your own.
A AAAAI & NIAID clinical guidance
2
Cut mammalian meat completely
Beef, pork, lamb, venison, bison, goat and rabbit all carry alpha-gal. Poultry, fish and shellfish don't — they stay on the plate.
A CDC clinical overview
3
Learn the hidden sources — gelatin first
Gelatin is mammal collagen: gummies, marshmallows, some capsule shells, broths and gravies. Tallow, lard and suet count too. Dairy affects some people and not others.
A− CDC & NIAID
4
Stop the next tick bite
This is the step that changes your trajectory. Further bites can push alpha-gal IgE back up and undo months of progress. Permethrin-treated clothing, EPA-registered repellent, and a full tick check within two hours of coming inside.
A CDC tick-bite prevention
5
Keep total histamine load down on reactive days
Some people find flares are milder when they go easy on other histamine-heavy foods while they're already reacting. Treat it as a comfort measure — it is not a substitute for avoidance.
B Evidence summary — not guideline-backed
6
Retest your IgE over time — talk to your doctor first
Alpha-gal IgE can fall over months to years once the bites stop, and some people regain tolerance. Your allergist is the only one who can tell you whether — and when — anything comes back on the plate.
A− NIAID & AAAAI

Two variants below — a supplement step that links into Lab Reports, and a behaviour step that carries the red CHECK FIRST card and clears up the carrageenan myth.

dark · supplement step · links to /lab
Omega-3 (EPA+DHA)

Heart & circulation · step 2 of 5

How to take it
1–2 g combined EPA + DHA a day, with a meal that has some fat in it — that's what it needs to absorb. Split the dose if 2 g at once sits badly. Give it 8–12 weeks before you judge it; triglycerides move slowly.
Pick a good one
Fish oil is where quality actually separates products — oxidation is the thing to look for, and it isn't on the label. Check the Purity Score and Evidence Grade before you buy.
Open Lab Reports
Evidence · A−
American Heart Association science advisory on omega-3 fatty acids and triglyceride lowering. REDUCE-IT trial of icosapent ethyl (New England Journal of Medicine, 2019). Cochrane systematic review of omega-3 for cardiovascular prevention.
Check first
High-dose omega-3 thins the blood a little. If you take a blood thinner, or you have surgery coming up, clear the dose with your doctor before you start.
Wellness information · Not medical advice
Never a replacement for your doctor.
light · behaviour step · myth-buster + check first
Reading a label

Alpha-gal syndrome · step 3 of 6

How to do it
Scan for the four words that matter most: gelatin, tallow, lard, suet. Then the quieter ones — beef or pork broth, collagen, and capsule shells on supplements and medicines. Tell every prescriber and pharmacist you have alpha-gal; some capsules and surgical products are mammal-derived.
One thing you can stop worrying about
Carrageenan is seaweed, not mammal. It contains no alpha-gal. It gets passed around in alpha-gal circles because it's a galactose polymer, but avoiding it isn't part of any standard guidance — don't spend your label-reading attention there.
Evidence · A−
CDC — Alpha-gal Syndrome, clinical overview and patient guidance. NIH / NIAID — alpha-gal syndrome research and food allergy guidance.
Check first
If you have ever had trouble breathing, throat tightness, faintness or a whole-body rash after eating, that is anaphylaxis territory. You need an allergist — and, if they prescribe one, an epinephrine auto-injector you carry.
Wellness information · Not medical advice
Never a replacement for your doctor.

06 — what I need from you before I build

  1. Layout and spacing — approve, or tell me what's off. Card radii, row height, gutter and the two-up desktop grid are the things to look at.
  2. Step detail: modal or route? I've mocked the centered modal and recommend it. Say the word if you want the third route instead.
  3. Alpha-gal copy — read steps 1–6 in section 03 closely. That's shipping copy, and it's the condition with the most legal and safety exposure in the section.
  4. Evidence grade format — I've used institution names (A · CDC clinical overview) rather than invented study counts. Confirm that's the format you want across all eight.
  5. Sidebar icon — HeartPulse shown. Easy to swap.
One flag before I write it. Alpha-gal is an anaphylaxis risk, not a wellness topic, and it's the first condition in this section where a reader could be harmed by acting on a page instead of seeing a doctor. I've handled it with the allergist banner above the protocol, doctor-forward language in two step titles, and a CHECK FIRST card naming anaphylaxis symptoms directly. If you want it heavier still — a hard interstitial before the protocol renders, or the protocol collapsed until acknowledged — tell me now; it's much cheaper to build in than to retrofit.
