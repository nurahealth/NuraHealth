NŪRA insight

Blood oxygen — SpO₂ — is the percentage of your red blood cells carrying oxygen to your tissues, and for most healthy people it sits between 95 and 100%. NŪRA reads it overnight, when your breathing is steadiest, so a single night reflects how well you were oxygenating while you slept. A brief dip into the low 90s now and then is normal; what matters is the pattern. Frequent or sustained dips below 90%, especially paired with loud snoring, gasping, or daytime fatigue, can be a sign of disrupted breathing during sleep and are worth raising with a doctor. Altitude, a stuffy nose, or sleeping on your back can nudge it down temporarily, too. Your readings have stayed comfortably in the healthy range with steady breathing — a good sign your recovery isn't being undercut overnight.
